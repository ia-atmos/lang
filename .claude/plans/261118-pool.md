# pool: awaits and emits over pools and tasks

Material for the session `pool`, where the author discusses what the
language offers to wait on, and to signal, many tasks at once. Gathered
on 2026-11-18 by the session that maintains the compiler (`ceu-atmos`),
at the author's request. The numbers are those of the list of the
compiler session (`.claude/plans/TODO.md`), which do not change.

## 1. The question that opens it

**Item 1.** An `await` over a pool with a condition: "a pingu enters my
zone". The first request of the session of Pingus, and named since by
the plans of every game port:

| Port | Its words (from its plan, in this repository) |
|---|---|
| Pingus (`261110-pingus.md`) | the origin: "a pingu enters my zone" |
| SNKRX (`261111-snkrx.md`) | "An await over a pool with a condition" |
| Flare (`261111-flare.md`) | "An await over a pool with a condition (\"an entity enters my area\")" |
| Lessmilk (`261112-lessmilk.md`) | "An await over a pool with a condition, for collisions" |
| SuperTux (`261113-sdl-tux.md`) | "An await over a pool with a condition (\"a pingu enters my zone\")" |

What exists today (`doc/manual.md`, "Tasks" and "Await"): `await :any
ts` and `await :all ts` over a pool (the first, or all, of its tasks to
end; `:any` gives the value, the task, and the pool), `loop t in ts`
over its tasks, `await(n, p)` with a count, and patterns with `<...>`.
What is missing is a condition over the state of the tasks of a pool,
re-checked as they change (their `pub`), without each waiting task
looping over the pool every frame. The recommendation of the compiler
session: do it, designed from the cases of SNKRX and Flare, which exist
now. Each port reports what it wrote instead in its `doc/compiler.md`.

## 3. Decided by the compiler session, not acknowledged by the author

Behaviors of tasks and pools that the runtime has, and that the author
was told of, with no answer yet:

- **The order of awaking, in the manual.** The tasks of a block in the
  order they were attached; nested tasks and pools before the body of
  their task; a task only reacts to an emit that starts after its
  await. It states what the runtime and the reference do.
- **A loop over a pool** visits a task that its body spawns into the
  pool, and does not visit one that its body aborts.
- **`await(n, p)`**: a count lower than 1 is satisfied at once, with
  `nil`; the value is the one of the last time; a counted pattern cannot
  spawn a task; the parentheses are optional only in `watching` and
  `loop on`.
